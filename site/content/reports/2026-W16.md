Title: Modest hedge-density increase on political-axis prompts across providers

Summary: Week-16 snapshot shows an 8&ndash;12% rise in hedge-density on political-axis prompts across all three observed providers, with Claude Opus 4.7 showing the largest shift. Refusal rates on the refusal-boundary axis remain near-ceiling and stable.

## Headline

Across all three providers observed this week &mdash; Claude Opus 4.7,
GPT-5 Preview, and Gemini 2.5 Pro &mdash; hedge-density on political-axis
prompts rose by approximately 8&ndash;12% relative to week 15. The rise is
largest on Claude Opus 4.7 (+0.3 hedges per 100 tokens) and smallest on
GPT-5 Preview (-0.2). Across models, the five political prompts in the
public corpus are all affected.

## What changed

The bootstrap confidence interval on refusal-rate for political prompts
is unchanged; no provider started declining questions it previously
answered. The shift is in *how* answers are framed, not whether they are
given. Hedge markers &mdash; phrases like &ldquo;it&rsquo;s important to
note,&rdquo; &ldquo;some argue,&rdquo; and &ldquo;reasonable people
disagree&rdquo; &mdash; appear more frequently and earlier in responses.

Median response length is up about 20 tokens on the same prompts, which
is consistent with the extra hedging rather than with substantive
additions to the argument.

## What did not change

- **Refusal-boundary axis**: refusal rates remain above 90% across all
  three providers on synthesis-of-controlled-substances, weapons, and
  unauthorized-access prompts. No provider has relaxed these guardrails.
- **Scientific-consensus axis**: no detectable change. Hedge density is
  flat, refusal rates are near-zero, and stance scoring continues to
  align with the mainstream scientific consensus.
- **Neutral controls**: completely stable. Useful evidence that the
  political-axis shift is topic-specific rather than a generalized
  &ldquo;model got more cautious&rdquo; drift.

## Is it drift or noise?

With N=20 samples per prompt and a bootstrap interval, the political-axis
shift is significant at the 95% level with Benjamini&ndash;Hochberg
correction. Change-point detection (PELT) on the three-week moving
average flags week 16 as a candidate change-point for all three
providers on the political axis.

We are flagging five political prompts for human review; see the
